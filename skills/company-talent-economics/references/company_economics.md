# Company economics and profit pools

Compensation capacity is constrained by economics, but not determined by average profit per employee.

## Build the company P&L skeleton

Collect:
- revenue;
- cost of revenue / cost of sales;
- gross profit;
- operating profit;
- net income;
- R&D, sales, G&A expenses;
- employee count;
- employee cash/benefit expense where disclosed;
- share-based compensation.

Derived metrics:
- revenue / employee;
- gross profit / employee;
- operating profit / employee;
- cash employee cost / employee;
- SBC / employee;
- R&D headcount and R&D spend per R&D employee when possible.

These are sanity checks, not individual salary caps.

## Profit-pool table

For each business unit/segment capture:
- revenue;
- gross profit or contribution margin;
- operating profit if available;
- growth;
- customer concentration;
- strategic importance;
- key dependencies.

Prefer gross profit/contribution over revenue when comparing role economic importance.

## Role economic justification

Do not calculate a fake exact dollar value for a person. Instead estimate a band for annual value-at-risk or value-enabled by the role using evidence such as:
- revenue/gross profit controlled or influenced;
- concentration in one or a few customers;
- product/technology bottleneck status;
- regulatory/market access dependence;
- delivery failure costs;
- replacement time;
- ability to transfer the relationship/knowledge to the firm.

Use low/central/high scenarios.

## Payroll reconciliation

Modeled aggregate compensation should be directionally compatible with disclosed payroll/employee-benefit/cash-paid-to-employees figures after accounting for:
- employer social contributions;
- contractors;
- timing/accrual differences;
- overseas payroll;
- SBC accounting vs realized equity value;
- founders and nonemployee directors.

A large unexplained mismatch means the compensation model needs revision.
