# Leadership allocation regimes

Model leaders by repeated, observable resource-allocation behavior. Do not diagnose personality or use cultural stereotypes.

Represent the implicit objective as weights on `growth, free cash flow, margin, market share, strategic option value, control, stability/reputation, policy mandate`.

## Evidence

Collect budget/headcount allocation across cycles, executive compensation metrics, board and ownership, responses to misses, acquisitions/divestitures/product closures, restructuring sequence, executive turnover, redeployment, consistency between statements and decisions, and decision centralization.

## Regime patterns

### Founder-centralized experimentation

Expect high variance across ventures, sponsor-driven reversals, and whole-project exits. Product sponsorship and technical reuse may outweigh annual plans.

### CFO/operator margin discipline

Expect productivity targets, layer reduction, location arbitrage, vendor consolidation, and function-level cost benchmarking.

### PE/integration discipline

Expect time-bounded EBITDA/synergy plans, duplicate-function cuts, site consolidation, management changes, and faster cash realization.

### Sales/customer-led allocation

Expect protection for bookings, renewals, and delivery; long-cycle research and weak-conversion products face scrutiny.

### Engineering/product-led allocation

Expect protection for technical bottlenecks and core product; peripheral operations, duplicate platforms, and weak commercialization may be cut first.

### Formal process/consensus governance

Expect slower decisions, documented consultation, defined selection pools, and more redeployment. Local law and works councils may dominate timing.

### Public-mandate/stability regime

Expect hiring freezes, attrition, transfer, contractor reduction, and non-renewal more often than abrupt layoffs.

### Forced-distribution performance regime

Expect persistent individual risk without a company event. Calibrate performance exits separately from restructuring.

## Scoring discipline

Use leadership as a conditional-path modifier, not the event trigger. Formal notices, cost targets, product funding, customer obligations, and operating evidence dominate speeches and reputation.

When evidence conflicts, report a mixed regime, widen timing/selection intervals, and cite the decisions supporting each interpretation.
