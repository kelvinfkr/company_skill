# Profit-to-Headcount economic model

## Classify the action

- **Survival cut:** close a cash/runway/covenant gap quickly.
- **Margin reset:** meet a stated EBITDA, operating-margin, or productivity target.
- **Capital reallocation:** move resources from low-return or low-option businesses into stronger pools.
- **Integration:** remove duplicate functions, products, sites, or layers after M&A.
- **Demand-capacity reset:** align shifts, sites, service capacity, or projects to demand.
- **Performance-system exit:** continuing relative-performance mechanism rather than episodic restructuring.

## Pressure model

Estimate ranges:

`cash gap = debt service + capex + operating cash burn - available liquidity - credible financing`

`cost gap = target operating profit - forecast operating profit - feasible non-labor savings`

`required labor saving = max(0, cash gap or cost gap allocation to labor)`

Account for severance, notice, vendor substitution, fixed facilities, knowledge transfer, and implementation lag. Payroll is not always avoidable within the horizon.

## Profit-pool table

Decompose at the most decision-relevant level:

`product x geography x channel x customer type x lifecycle`

Prefer contribution profit and cash conversion, then gross profit/working capital, then segment operating profit. Use revenue only when no better denominator exists.

For shared platforms, allocate value by supported pools and value at risk rather than arbitrary revenue shares.

## Business-unit value

`BU value = PV(future contribution) + strategic option + synergy + mandate value - follow-on investment - execution risk`

Classify each pool as cash engine, funded growth engine, mature harvest business, protected strategic/regulated capability, funded option, failed/unfunded option, or integration duplicate.

Current loss does not imply abandonment. Current profit does not imply safety when management is harvesting or exiting.

## Dependency and net benefit

Map `profit pool -> process -> product/project/customer/site -> team -> role -> employment bucket`.

Estimate value at risk, time to damage, minimum staffing, overlap, transfer/automation/outsourcing, recovery time, knowledge concentration, constraints, and cost avoidability.

Use:

`layoff net benefit = PV(avoidable loaded cost) - PV(lost contribution + execution risk + rehiring + knowledge + option value)`

Never claim private-person contribution with false precision.

## Company-type routing

- **Software/platform:** recurring gross profit, retention, compute/service cost, acquisition cost, reliability, concentration.
- **Hardware/manufacturing:** SKU/channel contribution, warranty/returns, inventory, utilization, shifts, yield, lifecycle.
- **Marketplace/advertising:** take rate, transaction contribution, liquidity, traffic acquisition, trust/safety, network effects.
- **Professional services:** backlog, utilization, billing rate, project margin, bench, client concentration.
- **Bank/insurance:** risk-adjusted return, capital use, loss/credit costs, regulatory staffing, branch/channel economics.
- **Biotech/R&D:** milestone funding, technical/regulatory probability, partnership economics, patent life, next financing point.
- **Startup:** runway, financing probability, burn multiple, contribution trajectory, customer concentration, board priorities.
- **PE/high leverage:** debt service, covenants, EBITDA plan, synergy target, asset sales, sponsor horizon.
- **Public/nonprofit/SOE:** appropriations, grants, service demand, mandated staffing, establishment rules, redeployment. Profit may not be the objective.

## Sanity checks

Reconcile implied labor savings with announced targets. Confirm costs are avoidable. Preserve minimum operations. Avoid revenue/contribution confusion, platform double counting, loss/abandonment confusion, and misreading skill/location substitution as broad hiring.
